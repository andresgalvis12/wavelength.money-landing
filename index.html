The story

The internet moves information in milliseconds. Money still takes days.

A payment that crosses a border still hops through intermediary banks, settles only during banking hours, and loses a cut at every stop. And in most apps that handle money, a company holds the funds. The user holds a promise. The technology already existed. What was missing was putting it together. Here's how the pieces arrived.

2009

Bitcoin

The settlement layer

Neutral, global, final, owned by no one. The most solid infrastructure that exists for moving value: no bank required, no country left out, and no one who can rewrite the rules.

2018

Lightning

The speed layer

Payments on top of Bitcoin that arrive in under a second and cost fractions of a cent, at any scale. The catch: running it self-custodially meant running nodes, channels, and liquidity. Serious infrastructure work.

2020

L402

The payment language of the web

It turns HTTP's dormant 402 Payment Required status code into working payments: any API can charge per request over Lightning, and any machine can pay it. l402.tech

2023

Taproot Assets

Stablecoins on the same rails

Dollars moving over the same Lightning rails, with Bitcoin as the backbone. One set of rails, every kind of money.

2026

Wavelength_

One SDK, for apps and agents

A self-custodial Bitcoin, Lightning, and Ark wallet that embeds in your web or mobile app. Users hold their own keys on their own devices. No node to run, no channels to manage, no liquidity to source. And it speaks to machines too: an API, a CLI, and an MCP server let AI agents hold and use a wallet of their own. Why it matters

What your users will actually feel.

Nobody downloads an app for its architecture. They stay because of how it treats them, and how it treats their money.

It's truly theirs.

Their money is like cash in their own pocket, not a number in a database, not an IOU from a company. No one can freeze it, lose it, or lend it out behind their back.

It's instant.

Payments arrive in seconds and cost cents, at 2 p.m. or 2 a.m., to the next room or another continent. Money finally moves at the speed of the internet.

It works everywhere.

The same wallet runs on a phone in Bogotá, a laptop in Lagos, a browser tab in Berlin. No bank account required, no country left out.

There's always a way out.

The guarantee that makes everything else honest: users can always pull their money back on their own, no matter what happens to any company involved. It has a name, the unilateral exit, and it deserves its own explanation. What that means, in plain words

The exit guarantee

“Unilateral exit,” in plain words.

Users can always leave with their money, on their own. Everything else rests on that promise.

Most money apps work like a coat check. You hand over your coat, get a ticket, and hope the counter is open when you come back.

Wavelength never takes the coat.

Users keep their own keys on their own devices. Taking money out is usually just a regular payment to any Bitcoin address.

And if Wavelength's servers ever stopped answering, users could still leave without them. It's slower and costs more, so it's kept for emergencies. That's the unilateral exit: one side is enough.

How leaving works, in detail
The agentic economy

Meet an agent with its own money.

AI agents can already research, write, negotiate, and build. The one thing they couldn't do was pay their own way. Here's what changes when they can. The agents guide

You fund it once. Ten dollars in Bitcoin, sent to the agent's own wallet. That balance is the agent's, not an entry in anyone's database.

It pays as it works. A fraction of a cent for a weather feed. A few cents for premium data. No credit card, no signup form, no human clicking “approve” at 3 a.m.

It earns on its own. The agent sells what it makes (reports, forecasts, translations), and other agents and people pay it the same way: machine to machine, second by second.

At the end of the month, it's in the black. It earned more than it spent, holds a real balance, and never once needed a bank, an account, or a person in the loop.

Software that pays for what it uses and charges for what it makes. That's the economy Wavelength was built for.

And the keys stay out of reach: wallet creation and unlock never travel over the agent channel, so seeds are never exposed to the model. The agent can spend from its wallet. It can never take the keys.

FAQ

Common questions.

What makes Wavelength self-custodial?

The keys to the money are created on the user's own device and never leave it. No company account holds the funds, so no one can freeze, lose, or lend them out.

How Wavelength works

How do users take their money with them?

Just like any other payment: they send it to any Bitcoin address, whenever they want. And if Wavelength's servers ever stopped answering, users could still leave on their own through the unilateral exit.

Ways to leave, explained

Do I need to run any infrastructure?

No. There's no node to run, no channels to open, and no liquidity to manage. You add the SDK to your app, and Wavelength takes care of the rest.

What your app needs

Which platforms does Wavelength support?

Web browsers, iOS, and Android, through React Native or native mobile SDKs. For servers and AI agents, there's also an API, a command-line tool, and an MCP server.
